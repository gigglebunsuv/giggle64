Key hardware behaviors to emulate:
    1. Texture memory and filtering (most important)
The RDP only had 4 KB of Texture Memory (TMEM). Practical color textures were usually 32×32 or 64×64 maximum (smaller with higher color depth or when using palettes/TLUT). Textures were frequently stretched across larger surfaces.
Crucially, the RDP did not use standard 4-point bilinear filtering. It used 3-point (triangular) bilinear filtering. This creates a distinctive hexagonal/"rupee" sampling pattern instead of the usual diamond pattern. Implement texture sampling that approximates this 3-point triangular interpolation rather than modern bilinear or anisotropic filtering. Also support optional mipmapping/trilinear behavior in the style of the RDP's 2-cycle mode. 
    2. Resolution and output
Render at a low internal resolution typical of the N64 (commonly 320×240, sometimes slightly higher). Upscale cleanly to the window without modern sharpening or high-quality upscalers that fight the soft look. The Video Interface (VI) also applied additional processing (including a dither filter / dedither and horizontal blending on many games), which further softened the image. Apply a mild overall softness consistent with this. 
    3. Color depth and dithering
The RDP pipeline was 32-bit internally, but the framebuffer was frequently 16-bit (RGBA5551 / 5-5-5-1). This caused color banding that was mitigated by hardware ordered dithering. Simulate quantization to ~15–16-bit color with ordered dithering so gradients have the characteristic subtle noise/pattern of real N64 output rather than clean modern banding or smooth 24-bit gradients. 
    4. Anti-aliasing and edges
The RDP had hardware edge anti-aliasing that used coverage bits. Edges were softer than pure aliased polygons but still distinctly low-resolution. Avoid modern MSAA or FXAA that looks too clean; keep a slightly soft, low-res edge quality. 
    5. Shading, lighting, and fog
Lighting was primarily Gouraud (per-vertex) shading calculated on the RSP. Use vertex lighting / Gouraud-style shading rather than per-pixel lighting. Support per-pixel fog (the RDP Blender stage computed fog as a function of range). 
    6. Geometry and overall constraints
Keep polygon counts low. Characters and objects should feel low-poly. Prefer vertex colors and simple textures over high-detail maps. Perspective-correct texturing is correct (the N64 had it; the PS1 did not), so do not introduce affine warping/wobble. 
    7. Overall aesthetic
The combination of low-resolution textures + 3-point triangular bilinear filtering + 16-bit dithered color + low output resolution + edge AA + VI softening produces the classic soft, slightly hazy, "vaseline" N64 look with characteristic texture blur. Aim for that rather than sharp pixel art or PS1-style nearest-neighbor blockiness.
