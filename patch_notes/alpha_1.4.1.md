# Giggle 64 — Alpha 1.4.1: Song of Lothelye

Lothelye's legend now has its own music, and the scene plays to it.

## The legend, scored

- **Its own piece of music.** The legend now has a five-minute score written
  for it, one section for each part of the story, with a proper ending.
  - Lothelye has her own theme. It plays when she appears at the pedestal,
    in the minor when she speaks of the dark before the world, and again
    when she says goodbye.
  - The world's birth gets a rising swell. Each goddess gets her own key and
    instrument: Alan has a pastoral oboe, Aphiel an airy flute and celesta,
    and Serielye horns and timpani.
  - Denangdyn gets a creeping figure in the low brass. The Nameless hero gets
    the main theme in a major key, and the full brass come in as the
    Magical Sword of Bun is made.
  - The main theme climbs a step for each of Gigglebuns the First, Second
    and Third. It comes home for **Gigglebuns the Fourth** with the whole
    orchestra.
- **The scene follows the music.** Every moment of the scene lands on its
  beat in the score: Lothelye appearing, each goddess's chime, the Dark
  Lord's roar, the stars falling into the blade, the last stroke, and the
  wake-up. While the music plays, the scene keeps time by the music itself,
  so the two never drift apart.
- **No more pressing A.** The legend's text boxes now move on by
  themselves. Each box stays up long enough to read, and the last one
  closes as the next moment begins.

## Changes

- The goddesses' chimes in the legend are now tuned to the music's chords.
- Some of Lothelye's longer lines are split over two boxes, so no box runs
  past three lines.
- The dream's choir hum is quieter while the score plays.

## For the composer

- MIDI: `_giggle64_work/music/lothelye_legend.mid`. Render it to
  `assets/music/lothelye_legend.ogg` (or .mp3 / .wav). Until that file
  exists, the scene keeps the same timing silently.
- Keep the rendered audio the same length and tempo as the MIDI. The scene
  waits on the score's cue times, and the cue names are marked in the
  MIDI's markers.
